# AGENTS.md

Pass-success models. Recording a finished round and pushing it are root rules in `AGENTS.md`.

## Always

- The destination of a pass is the teammate the passer aimed at, read from the kick by `soccervalue/intent.py`. Do not score at the point the event file gives: on an unsuccessful pass that point is the opponent's next action, and the endpoint then carries the outcome. `results/pass-intent/conclusion.json` has the numbers for both endpoints on the same passes.
- The geometric rule in `soccervalue/intent.py` stays the endpoint estimator. A trained aimed-receiver model beats it on successful passes, 7 of 7 leave-one-match-out folds, but the extra wins come from preferring the more open teammate, and on the failed passes, where the endpoint has to be right, that preference is untested. `results/pass-intent-model/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- That worry was then measured and it did not bite: with the model endpoint the spatial features keep their sign and their gain on all 21 leave-one-match-out folds. The model endpoint is safe, and it is also no better: the linear endpoint is a wash and the MLP endpoint is worse. A better aimed receiver does not give a better pass-success model. `results/pass-intent-endpoint/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- The bottleneck of the pass-success model is not the endpoint. Failures are 9% of the passes and plain geometry already takes most of the learnable signal. Do not keep changing the endpoint; change what the model is asked to predict.
- The world model and the value model are joined in `soccervalue/pass_value.py`. The successor state the world model produces carries the right value: the gap to the recorded successor is 0.008 against 0.020 for the pre-pass state. Do not score a pass with the simulated successor yet: its value change separates in only 5 of 7 matches.
- Score a pass with the value change, not with the absolute value. The absolute value after a successful pass and after a failed one are nearly the same. `results/pass-value/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- The weak ownership weight on the against head of the value model is not a defect to fix. The relation is not identifiable from seven matches: the measured conditional rates for the opponent change sign between the splits, 0.79, 0.43 and 1.84. Forcing the two heads to be symmetric with a football prior does not make the absolute value separate, 4 of 7 matches, and it costs the against head 0.043 of test AUROC. Keep the two separate heads. `results/value-symmetric/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- The state samples now also carry the xG of the shots inside the window, not only whether a shot happened. The dataset annotates all 164 shots with their own xG, so the dense label needs no extra model. The graded label fixed the evaluated team's side, 3 of 3 splits, and did not fix the opponent's, which still has only 54, 11 and 11 windows with an opponent shot across the splits. The opponent side is out of events, not out of label density.
- `soccervalue/surface.py` holds a position value surface over eight zones. It is the first value signal in the project that does not read the outcome, so it can value a pass that was never played. On the held-out match the centre band beats the wide band in all four depth bands, and the gap grows towards the goal. Its value change separates successful from failed passes in 7 of 7 matches, but at a fifth of the state model's gap, because it cannot see who holds the ball at the end. Keep both: the state model for retrospective scoring, the surface for candidates. `results/value-surface/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- Eight zones is not a preference. The published error law for a surface of this kind puts this project at about eight zones with the 2920 successful moves in the training split, and a 16 by 12 grid would need some 5.4 Premier League seasons. Do not widen the grid without new data.
- The position surface values a zone. It is not a candidate ranker: on the held-out match it puts the player who received the ball first 19.6% of the time and the combined value times probability score 27.8%, against 76.0% for the geometric ray rule. Value and reachability are negatively correlated across candidates at -0.40, so multiplying them is worse than the probability alone. Keep the three jobs apart: the ray rule decides who, the success model decides whether it arrives, the surface decides what the zone is worth. `results/candidate-pass/conclusion.json` has the numbers.
- Model B, logistic regression on pass geometry plus a few hand-built spatial features, is the baseline. On the intent endpoint it beats plain geometry on validation and test, and on 7 of 7 leave-one-match-out folds. The first round's larger margin came from an endpoint that carried the result and is not comparable.
- The raster CNN still does not beat Model B. On the intent endpoint it is close (test AUROC 0.805 against 0.818). Do not add layers to it on the strength of the first round.
- The match split is fixed in `soccervalue/matches.py`. Do not retune it from test scores.
- `python -m soccervalue.run_pass_success` writes local artifacts under `outputs/pass_success/`. Copy the official conclusion to `results/pass-success/conclusion.json` before committing the round.
- `python -m soccervalue.run_pass_intent` writes local artifacts under `outputs/pass_intent/`. Copy the official conclusion to `results/pass-intent/conclusion.json` before committing the round.
